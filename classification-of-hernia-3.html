Classification of Hernia

What is a hernia?

A hernia is the protrusion of an organ or tissue through a weak point or opening in the wall that normally holds it.

Hernia is classified into 4 parts

  1. Based on sites
  2. According to contents
  3. Related to birth
  4. Clinical classification

Further classification

1. Based on sites

Where the hernia appears in the body.

Inguinal hernia

In the groin, through the inguinal canal. Indirect: at the opening of the canal. Direct: near the opening of the canal.

Femoral hernia

Occurs in the femoral canal, below the groin.

Obturator hernia

Through the obturator canal of the pelvis. Rare.

Diaphragmatic hernia

Abdominal contents pass through the diaphragm into the chest.

Lumbar hernia

In the lumbar region of the back. Rare.

Spigelian hernia

Through the abdominal wall at the outer edge of the rectus muscle. Rare.

Umbilical hernia

At the navel.

Epigastric hernia

In the upper abdomen, at the midline.

Hiatus hernia

The stomach pushes up through the opening (hiatus) of the diaphragm.

Incisional hernia

At the site of a previous surgical incision.

2. According to contents

What is inside the hernial sac.

Omentocele

The sac contains omentum.

Enterocele

The sac contains intestine.

Cystocele

The sac contains the urinary bladder.

Littre’s hernia

The sac contains a Meckel’s diverticulum.

Maydl’s hernia

Two loops of bowel lie in the sac, and the connecting part stays inside the abdomen.

Sliding hernia

An organ, such as the caecum or colon, forms part of the wall of the sac.

Richter’s hernia

Only part of the bowel wall is trapped in the sac.

3. Related to birth

When the hernia develops.

Congenital hernia

Present from birth. This is the common type.

Acquired hernia

Develops later in life.

4. Clinical classification

How the hernia behaves.

Reducible hernia

The contents can be pushed back into the abdomen.

Irreducible hernia

The contents cannot be pushed back, but the bowel is not blocked.

Obstructed hernia

The bowel in the hernia is blocked, but its blood supply is intact.

Inflamed hernia

The contents of the hernia are inflamed.

Strangulated hernia

The blood supply of the contents is cut off. This is a surgical emergency.
