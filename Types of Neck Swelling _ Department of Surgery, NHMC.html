Types of Neck Swelling

What is a neck swelling?

A neck swelling is a lump or mass in the neck.

Causes are grouped by how fast the swelling appears (acute, subacute or chronic), and by how common each cause is (common, uncommon or rare).

Neck swellings are grouped into 3 types

  1. 1. Acute
  2. 2. Subacute
  3. 3. Chronic
  4. Lymph node areas of the neck

Causes explained

1. Acute

Swellings that appear suddenly. Most are infections.

Common
  • Cytomegalovirus infection
  • Epstein-Barr virus infection
  • Staphylococcal or streptococcal infection
  • Toxoplasmosis
  • Viral upper respiratory infection
Uncommon
  • Acute sialadenitis
  • Arteriovenous fistula
  • Bartonella henselae infection
  • Hematoma
  • Human immunodeficiency virus infection
  • Mycobacterium tuberculosis infection
  • Parotid lymphadenopathy
  • Pseudoaneurysm
Rare
  • None listed

2. Subacute

Swellings that appear over weeks.

Common
  • Squamous cell carcinoma of the upper aerodigestive tract
Uncommon
  • Amyloidosis
  • Lymphoma
  • Metastatic cancer
  • Parotid tumor
  • Sarcoidosis
  • Sjögren syndrome
Rare
  • Castleman disease
  • Kikuchi disease
  • Kimura disease
  • Rosai-Dorfman disease

3. Chronic

Swellings that stay for a long time.

Common
  • Thyroid pathology
Uncommon
  • Branchial cleft cyst
  • Carotid body tumor
  • Glomus jugulare tumor
  • Glomus vagale tumor
  • Laryngocele
  • Lipoma
  • Thyroglossal duct cyst
Rare
  • Liposarcoma
  • Parathyroid carcinoma

4. Lymph node areas of the neck

A swollen node in an area often points to a source nearby.

Submental triangle (submental nodes)

Rarely involved early, except from cancer of the lip.

Submandibular triangle (submandibular group)

Anterior two-thirds of the tongue, floor of the mouth, gums and the lining of the cheek.

Upper jugular chain, or jugulodigastric area

Posterior auricular nodes. Linked to the nasopharynx.

Midjugular chain (deep lateral cervical nodes)

Any part of the oral cavity, pharynx or larynx, especially growths in the Waldeyer ring (nasopharynx, tonsil, base of the tongue).

Lower jugular chain (supraclavicular nodes)

Thyroid, piriform sinuses and upper oesophagus. Rarely from a primary below the clavicle.

Posterior triangle lymph nodes

Nasopharynx, posterior scalp, ear, temporal bone or skull base.
